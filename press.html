The Gentle Hand of Atta

Twenty-seven years on one patch of land, and a refusal to hate.

Feature Documentary/ Palestine · United Kingdom/ Year TBD/ Runtime TBD

Logline

A Palestinian farmer has spent his life on the same land in the occupied West Bank — and his life documenting it, one demolition at a time, without letting the anger become hate.

Synopsis

Atta Jaber has farmed the same land in the Baqa’a Valley of the occupied West Bank his whole life. For twenty-seven years, filmmaker Bruno Sorrentino has returned to document it: homes demolished, land seized, water supplies destroyed. Through all of it, Atta’s words hold steady — “I have no hate. If I let anger turn to hate, something inside me will die.”

Across four generations of one family, The Gentle Hand of Atta bears witness to a quiet, sustained resistance and a deliberate refusal to hate. Shot over decades in the same valley, it is a portrait of endurance measured not in events but in seasons — ploughing, olive harvests, rebuilding — and of what it costs, and what it preserves, to meet dispossession without bitterness.
